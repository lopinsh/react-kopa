# Principles

How the values become stances for the product. Each principle names the values it serves and what it means in practice, so it can settle a real decision.

## The principles

### Calm, not sticky

*Serves presence.* The site never tries to keep people scrolling. It shows what is new, then lets people go.

- **So we** notify only about things that need the person: a reply, a decision, a change to their event.
- **We don't** use streaks, endless feeds, "you might also like", or counts that exist only to look busy.

### Make the first step small

*Serves courage, connection.* Reaching out should feel as light as possible, for an introvert too.

- **So we** keep one short form to join or ask a question, and show group size so people can pick a small group.
- **We don't** ask for long applications, show who was declined, or make anyone feel judged for asking.

### Find your people at a glance

*Serves belonging.* Before reading a name, a person should know what kind of group it is, where, and how big.

- **So we** show category colour and name, city and member count on every group card, bar and chat label.
- **We don't** let a name stand alone (an organisation's name says nothing about what it does), or add badges that compete with those signals.

### Room to be yourself

*Serves being yourself.* No one is ranked or performing here.

- **So we** let people choose what their profile shows, and keep members-only things among members.
- **We don't** show likes, follower counts, popularity or activity scores of people.

### Safe to meet strangers

*Serves kindness and trust.* People meet in real life through this site. Trust matters more than reach.

- **So we** let strangers reach you only through a group, and make blocking and reporting easy to find.
- **We don't** show private data to logged-out visitors, or let anyone message anyone.

### Honest, not hollow

*Serves trust.* What you see works. What does not exist yet is hidden.

- **So we** hide what a person can't use, and say plainly what happened after every action.
- **We don't** show placeholders, "coming soon", dead links, fake numbers or a button the server will refuse.

### Easy to start something

*Serves passing it on.* Anyone with a spark should be able to start a group and run it without being an expert.

- **So we** keep creating and running a group simple, and give organisers what they need to welcome people: requests, announcements, events.
- **We don't** bury organisers in settings they never asked for.

### Local and in your language

*Serves belonging.* Made for people in Latvia: Latvian first, with full English.

- **So we** use Latvian conventions in both languages (Monday first, 24-hour time, Riga time) and keep every text in both.
- **We don't** let the browser decide formats, or mix languages on one screen.

### Simple beats clever

*Serves all of them, over time.* Built by one person in spare time. It has to stay small enough to care for.

- **So we** use one component for one job, and remove features that don't serve the loop.
- **We don't** build a second way to do the same thing.

## How it should feel

- **Content first, calm, obvious.** It should "just make sense" without explanation.
- **Understand a group at a glance**, before reading the title: colour = category, city, member count (an introvert may prefer a small group). These signals are the heart of a card; everything that competes with them is noise.
- **Trust matters.** People meet in real life through this. Moderation (tag approval and merging, reports) keeps content tidy and findable; later, reputation or vouching (Couchsurfing-style) may help.
- **Honest, not hollow.** No fake placeholders or dead links. Hide things until they exist.
